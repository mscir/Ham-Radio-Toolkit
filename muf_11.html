Propagation Primer for New Hams

What is an Ionosonde?

An ionosonde is a specialized high-power radar station on the ground that shoots rapid sweeps of radio frequencies straight up into space (Vertical Incidence). By recording exactly how long it takes for each frequency to echo back to Earth, it calculates the precise height and electron density of the ionosphere's reflecting layers at that moment. The Global Ionosonde Feed Average column on this page automatically pools live radar readings from over 80 active stations worldwide to build a dynamic planet-wide benchmark.

What is the Kp Index? (e.g., Kp 2.0)

The Kp Index measures real-time geomagnetic activity on Earth driven by solar winds. It scales from 0 to 9. A quiet, stable space climate sits around Kp 0 to 2. When the index crosses Kp 4+, a geomagnetic storm is occurring. For HF operators, high Kp values indicate that the ionosphere is becoming unstable or depleted, causing high-frequency signals to shoot into space instead of bouncing back down.

What is foF2? (Critical Frequency)

The foF2 is the absolute highest frequency that the F2 layer of the ionosphere can reflect back to Earth if a radio signal is shot straight up vertically into the sky. If you transmit higher than the foF2 frequency straight up, it pierces the layer and goes out to space. Because it represents the baseline density of electrons in the ionosphere, it is the foundational starting number for all skywave propagation models.

What is the MUF? (Maximum Usable Frequency)

The MUF is the highest frequency that can be used for communication along a specific oblique path between two distant points. Because long-distance signals hit the ionosphere at a shallow, glancing angle instead of straight up, the layer can bounce much higher frequencies than the baseline foF2. As a rule of thumb, the maximum possible single-hop MUF is roughly 3 times the local foF2 baseline.

How the Calculation Works

This calculator merges real-world space environment physics with orbital geometry. First, it queries live 3-hour geomagnetic values from NOAA and active radar sounder baselines from global ionosondes using custom JSON infrastructure. It then scales the baseline foF2 downward if a geomagnetic storm is present. Next, it identifies how many geometric skips (hops) are needed to cover your path distance (since an F2 hop peaks at 3,500 km). Finally, it applies the Secant Law of Curved-Earth Oblique Incidence Geometry to project exactly how much higher your grazing transmission angle boosts your usable frequency spectrum.
